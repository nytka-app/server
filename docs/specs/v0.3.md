# Nytka v0.3: offline sync

**Status: draft.** The whole picture lives in [../vision.md](../vision.md). The chunk format and the
HTTP API stay as [v0.1.md](v0.1.md) fixes them. This spec covers both repositories,
`nytka-app/android` and `nytka-app/server`.

Version 0.3 pulls the audio the pendant stored while the phone was away. It adds no wire format and
no endpoint, and it cannot recover audio lost in link stalls, which the pendant never stores (see
The pendant). "v0.3" is the roadmap milestone, not a release number: the server is at 0.3.0 and the
app at 0.3.1, so the work ships in whichever minor release release-please cuts next.

## Done when

1. The pendant is worn and the phone stays out of Bluetooth range for 30 minutes of talking, then
   comes back. Within 10 minutes of the reconnect every spoken sentence is in a conversation, each
   conversation starts within 3 seconds of when it was said, and no text appears twice.
2. Killing the app, or switching Bluetooth off, in the middle of a sync and starting again finishes
   the sync without repeated text. On firmware 3.0.20 the interruption loses no audio. On 3.0.21 it
   may cost up to 45 records, about 4 seconds (Delete semantics, Sync flow 4).
3. With the server unreachable during and after a sync, the audio waits in the queue and the app sends
   no `ADVANCE` for it. When uploads succeed, `ADVANCE` follows for the acknowledged part only.
4. While a backlog of 20 minutes or more syncs, new speech still gets a transcript within three
   minutes, and the link shows under 1% more lost notifications than in the five minutes before.
5. Muted for 10 minutes with the phone away and the wearer talking: none of that speech reaches the
   server, and the pendant's storage is empty afterwards.
6. On firmware below 3.0.20, or with the storage feature bit clear, the Device tab says why and the app
   writes nothing to the storage service. Live capture is unaffected.
7. With the queue over 50% of its cap the sync waits and drops no chunk; it resumes below 40%.
8. A stored session older than an open conversation joins the conversation its capture time falls
   in, a batch within the gap of two conversations merges them, and a live batch is transcribed
   before a backlog's. `apiVersion` stays `1`.
9. The capture-time tests pass on synthetic records built with the firmware's packing rule, a clock
   restart and a ring reset included. On the device, frame times land within 2 seconds of a reference.

## Scope

**In:** the pendant clock write; the ring sync (protocol, capture times, queue, resume, `ADVANCE`
after the server's answer); the mute filter; the Device tab card; the server's job priority and
merge.

**Out:** audio lost in link stalls (never stored); firmware below 3.0.20; `CLEAR` from the app; Wi-Fi
retrieval (the consumer build leaves it off); firmware updates; importing Omi's cloud audio; iOS.

## Wire format

Stored audio uses `application/vnd.nytka.frames.v1` unchanged, sent to `POST /api/v1/chunks`, with
no flag and no new field: the header has no spare bits (`version` 1, `codec` 21), a flag would cost
a version 2, and the server handles the frames the same either way. What changes is how a session
and a capture time are made.

- **Session.** One new capture session per sync run (each connection), and another at each pendant
  clock restart (below). Sequence numbers count stored frames from 0 without holes: a hole makes the
  server wait 10 minutes (`ProcessSessionHandler.GapWait`), and a late chunk is trimmed.
- **Limits and order.** 1,500 frames and 512 KiB a chunk, as before; a chunk may span hours. The
  uploader sends live chunks before stored ones, each oldest first, so a backlog never delays new
  speech.

### Capture times

The v0.1 rule (the phone's clock when the frame's last fragment arrived) holds for live frames only.
A stored frame's time is rebuilt from the record stamps. Records come in ring order. A run is a
stretch of records whose stamps differ by at most 1 second. Anchor each run at its first stamp
rollover: the record whose stamp first exceeds the run's first stamp ends at its stamp, `T * 1000`
ms, so frames before and in it are back-dated from there in 20 ms steps. After the anchor, `start =
max(cursor, T * 1000)`, frame `k` gets `start + 20 * k` ms and `cursor = start + 20 * n`. A run with
no rollover is left-aligned at its stamp. Starting the run at its first stamp instead would run
early, then snap forward, and the server reads a jump of 500 ms or more as silence
(`SpeechDetector`).

- A stamp more than 2 seconds below the previous record's marks a clock restart: begin a new segment,
  with a new session and no `cursor`.
- `setUp` reads the pendant clock (`19b10032`) before writing the phone's and exposes `skew`,
  pendant minus phone in seconds, as `PendantStorage.clockSkew`; the first INFO of the connection
  gives `writeSeq` at that write. The sync position persists the pair (`writeSeq`, `skew`), so a
  resumed sync reads it back instead of the clock, which then shows about 0. The last segment ran on
  the stale clock: when `|skew|` exceeds 5, subtract `skew` from the stamps of records below
  that `writeSeq`, which were stamped before the clock write (open decision 2).
- Drop, and count, records stamped more than 60 seconds ahead of the phone's clock.
- The error is about 0.15 seconds. A simulation of `write_to_storage` (97-byte frames, stamps cut to
  the second) puts every frame within 140 ms of the truth. The SD worker's delay adds to that on the
  device, and the rule assumes the audio clock and the RTC agree (50 ppm apart adds 0.2 s an hour to
  an unbroken run).

## App

`:pendant` speaks the protocol, `:core` rebuilds times and owns the queue, and `:app` runs the flow.
`:pendant` and `:core` stay independent and meet in `:app`.

### Sync flow

1. **Start.** On every `Connected` and on "Sync now", once `/api/v1/info` reports a `serverVersion`
   that carries migration `0005` (the server ships first; an older server runs a backlog FIFO ahead
   of live speech). Live capture keeps running during the sync, and the pendant records nothing new
   while connected, so no timer runs. `setUp` changes its order: codec check, audio subscription,
   pendant clock (read, then write), device info. `CaptureController` must set the audio intent from
   the persisted mute setting before it connects, not after `Connected` with its `muted = true`
   placeholder, so a service that starts as the phone comes back subscribes inside `setUp`. Until
   then the pendant drops frames.
2. **Check.** Before any write to the storage service, stop (Unsupported) if the firmware is under
   3.0.20 or the feature bit is clear. Then subscribe to control notifications, send stop and ask
   INFO (on status 9, retry every 2 s for 20 s); a `packetBytes` other than 444 is Unsupported. A
   `writeSeq` below the committed position, or a `droppedPackets` below the last one seen, means the
   ring was cleared: start a new epoch with `committedNext` at `readSeq`. A clear followed by a
   refill past `committedNext` with `droppedPackets` at 0 shows only in the stamps: if the first
   record read is stamped over 2 s before the last committed stamp (persisted in the position),
   treat it the same way.
3. **Read.** From `max(readSeq, committedNext)`, READ 2,000 packets (888 KB, about 2.7 minutes of
   audio) at a time; READ_BEGIN must follow within 10 s (5 s after the first window) and data at
   least every 10 s. Restart the record reassembler at each READ_BEGIN: stray DATA can follow the
   STOP ACK. Parse the records, assign times, drop what a mute covers, and repeat until
   `committedNext` reaches `writeSeq`. A `readSeq` above `committedNext` is audio the pendant no
   longer holds: count it as lost.
4. **Commit.** Frames are safe once Room holds them, as in v0.1. Commit every 20 records or 100 ms,
   whichever comes first, in one transaction with the position (`committedNext`, session, next frame
   number). That bounds the app's loss window at about 20 records; on 3.0.21 the firmware's early
   advance adds about 22 (Delete semantics): up to about 45 records, 4 s, per interruption.
5. **Wait.** Before each window, wait while the queue is over 50% of its cap (resume below 40%): a
   chunk dropped at the cap would be audio the pendant may already have freed. If the live stream
   loses over 5% of its notifications during a window, pause 30 seconds; after three pauses in a row
   stop until the next connection.
6. **Fail.** No READ_BEGIN, a DONE with status 9 or 10, or a stall: send stop, keep what was
   committed, retry after 30 seconds, 2 minutes, then every 10 minutes (PR #11578 reports SD read
   failures on 3.0.21). A lost link needs no handling: the next connection resumes from `committedNext`.
7. **Advance.** When no transfer runs, send `ADVANCE(ackedThrough)` if it exceeds what was last
   advanced, at most every 10 seconds. `ackedThrough` is the lowest ring sequence any stored frame
   or chunk still in the queue came from, or `committedNext` when none is. Only the server's `200`
   or `202` removes a stored chunk. On `400`, `409` or `413` (which the uploader treats as done for
   live chunks) a stored chunk is parked locally, out of the upload queue and still counted in
   `ackedThrough`, so the only copy is never freed; the Device tab shows the parked count. Status 10
   means the firmware is already past that point. The app never sends `CLEAR`.

### Throughput and battery

The pendant stores about 20 MB an hour of the time its mic was awake (444 bytes per 80 ms); a full
ring is about 25 hours of that. The Bluetooth rate is UNKNOWN: at 30 KB/s, a planning figure, an
hour of stored audio takes about 11 minutes. Developer mode shows the measured rate; if it is low,
try an MTU of 498, which the pendant offers, instead of the 247 the app requests now. The battery
cost is not measured: the link already runs at the interval the pendant requests for live audio, a
sync adds SD reads, and the mic stays awake until it ends. Diagnostics log the pendant's battery at
the start and end of each sync, so real runs give the cost per megabyte. There is no battery gate
and no Wi-Fi-only setting; stored chunks upload as live ones do.

### Mute

A mute promises that nothing is recorded, and the pendant cannot keep that promise while the phone
is away. So the app keeps a mute log (the time of every change, from the button, the notification
and the Device tab; a mute stays open until the next change, so it covers a whole absence) and drops
every stored frame within 2 seconds of a muted stretch. Dropped frames never enter the queue and
count as consumed for `ADVANCE`. The first-run consent text gains a sentence: the pendant also
records while the phone is away (open decision 1).

### Queue and database

Room moves to version 4 with an auto-migration and the committed schema, never a destructive
fallback. Track 2 picks the tables and keeps three rules. A commit is one transaction: the frames
and the position of the paired pendant (`committedNext`, epoch, advanced point, session, next frame number, last dropped count, last committed stamp, and the clock pair `writeSeq` and `skew`). Sealing never mixes live and stored frames, or two sessions, and a
stored chunk records its ring range; `Uploader` follows the order rule in Wire format. A `mute_log` table holds the mute changes, and parked chunks stay in a table of their own. `ChunkSource` and `FrameSink` gain members with default bodies, so the fakes in `DeveloperViewModelTest.kt` and `CaptureControllerTest.kt` still compile.

### Screens

The Device tab gets a **Pendant storage** card below the pendant card. Its state line reads "Nothing
stored" or "About 12 min stored. It syncs when connected." when idle; a progress bar and "Syncing
42%, 38 KB/s, about 3 min left" while syncing; "Waiting for uploads: the queue is 61% full.",
"Paused: the link dropped. It resumes when the pendant reconnects." or "The pendant did not answer
(status 9). Trying again in 2 min." when stuck; and "Offline sync needs firmware 3.0.20 or later;
this pendant has 3.0.19. Update it with the official Omi app." when unsupported. Below it: "Synced
today: 41 min"; "Lost: 12 min" when audio was overwritten or freed early; "Pendant clock was off by
6 min, times corrected" after a correction. **Sync now** shows when idle and connected, **Stop**
while syncing (it holds until the next connection or Sync now). The foreground notification appends
"· syncing 42%". There is no new alert: the three from v0.1 stay. Developer mode adds the ring
(`readSeq`, `writeSeq`, capacity, dropped), the last DONE status, the measured rate, live-stream
loss during a sync, frames dropped for a mute or a bad stamp, the clock skew and the segment count.
Diagnostics samples carry the same counters (additive; the server stores each sample as JSON).

## Server

Two changes: a job priority, in migration `0005_offline_sync.sql` (the number reserved for v0.3;
v0.4 keeps `0006` to `0008`), and the conversation merge. `apiVersion` stays 1.

**Priority.** `JobQueue.DequeueAsync` runs jobs in `run_after, id` order, so a backlog's
`process-session` and `transcribe` jobs would run ahead of later live ones and hold new speech back
for the length of the backlog (done when 4). The migration adds `jobs.priority` (smallint, default
0) and an index on `(priority, run_after, id)`, and `DequeueAsync` orders by `priority` first.
`EnqueueAsync` gains `priority` as a parameter with default 0, since v0.2 and v0.4 call it, and its
conflict clause becomes `do update set priority = least(jobs.priority, excluded.priority)`, because
the dedupe key would otherwise keep the first insert's priority. A job is queued at priority 1 when
its audio is late, meaning its last frame is over 5 minutes old when the chunk arrives
(`ChunkEndpoints`) or the batch is made (`ProcessSessionHandler`); the scheduler's `process-session`
re-queue (`Scheduler`) keeps the session's priority and other kinds stay at 0. The server infers
this, so the app sends no flag, and a drained offline queue yields to fresh speech.

**Merge.** `ConversationStore.AssignAsync` already places a batch by capture time, but takes only
one match (`order by ended_at desc limit 1`). Live speech that resumes when the phone returns often
arrives first, and a stored batch that falls between that conversation and an earlier stored one
then joins the live one and leaves one stretch of talk split in two. `AssignAsync` must take every
conversation within the gap, keep the one that starts first, move the others'
`transcription_batches`, `segments` and `speech_audio` rows to it (in that order, then delete the
emptied ones: `BatchStore.CompleteAsync` reads the conversation id inside its own transaction, so a
batch finishing mid-merge still lands in the survivor), set its times to cover all of them and the
batch, and open it, all under the advisory lock it already holds. Later schemas reference
`conversations` with `on delete cascade` (v0.2 `tasks`, v0.4 `memories` and `memory_runs`), so the
merge must also move those rows (on a repeated task fingerprint keep the row the user touched), keep
the survivor's `title`, and reset its `ai_status` and `memory_runs` row so both run again.

**Already right.** `Timeline.Decode` pushes an overlapping frame forward, so approximate times
cannot run backwards; `MaxChunksPerRun` (40 chunks, 20 minutes) bounds memory on a backlog;
retention keys on capture time (`DeleteSpeechAudioEndedBeforeAsync`), so old audio is deleted at the
next daily run and its transcript stays.

## Dedupe

Live and stored audio cannot overlap: the pendant stores only with no connection and streams only
with one (The pendant, "When audio is stored"). Whole-second stamps can make their times touch or
overlap by up to a second at a boundary, which is harmless: sessions process separately and
assignment merges by time. What can repeat is a re-read range: after an interruption the app skips
every record below `committedNext`, a retried chunk meets the server's duplicate rule (`200`, or
`409` when its range differs), and only losing the app's own state uploads an unadvanced range.

## Open decisions

Each has a recommendation. Settle it during implementation, with evidence.

1. **Mute while away.** Drop the stored audio recorded during a mute, or upload everything.
   Recommend dropping: the pendant cannot mute offline, so the app has to keep the promise.
2. **Clock after a restart.** Correct the last segment by the connection skew, or only log it.
   Recommend correcting when a backward stamp step marks the segment and `|skew|` exceeds 5 s.
3. **First-sync backlog.** Over 90,000 unread packets (about 2 hours): ask once, or import silently.
   Recommend asking, import by default; discarding sends `ADVANCE(writeSeq)` after a confirm.
4. **Window and throttle.** 2,000 packets and a 30 s pause above 5% live loss are guesses. Recommend
   shipping them, showing the measured rate and live loss in developer mode, and tuning (MTU 498
   first).
5. **3.0.21's early advance.** Accept up to about 45 lost records (4 s) per interruption: about 22
   from the firmware, which no command disables, and up to 20 from the app's commit window.
   Recommend accepting; the app assumes neither firmware behavior.
6. **Priority signal.** The server infers late audio from its age, or the app marks stored uploads
   with a header. Recommend inference: no protocol change, and it covers a drained offline queue.
